- **Fixed**: an I/O completion trigger destroyed by an exception now waits for the reads and writes it is still tracking. `~TCompletionTrigger` was empty, so a throw between submitting I/O and calling `Wait()` (a `TDiskFull` from a sorter spill in the middle of a merge, say) freed a stack-owned trigger that in-flight completions then called into. Today such a throw ends in `abort()`, so nothing reaches the dangling trigger; the merge retry that #590 needs would. The submit paths now always deliver a completion they have registered, even when the submit itself throws, so the wait can't hang. Each fiber also keeps its own C++ exception state across switches now. A fiber that parks mid-unwind (as this wait does, and as the index-file destructors from #593 already did on real disks) used to leak its in-flight exception to every other fiber on its runner. There, `std::uncaught_exceptions()` read one too high, and the index-file destructors rely on it to tell an abandoned build from a finished one (#590).

- **Fixed (undefined behavior)**: three UBSan findings. (1) A repo opened with `TTtl::max()` (the global repo; many tests) computed its close deadline as `now() + Ttl`, which overflows the nanosecond clock and, wrapped, lands about a second before `now()` for a repo meant to live forever. A new `Orly::AddTtl` saturates at the latest representable deadline, and the repo cache and the durable managers use it (#990). (2) `ParseRfc3339` overflowed converting a time past 2262 or before 1677 to the nanosecond clock; the input arrives from clients over the WebSocket (`try ... at time "..."`). Such a time now clamps to the nearest representable one, so a far-future `at time` is still "later than every version" (#991). (3) `TDiskController::TEvent` left `Kind` uninitialised, so tearing down a pooled event that was never used switched on garbage and could destroy a `std::function` that had never been constructed; it now starts as a kind with nothing to destroy, and returns to it after a reset so a second reset does not destroy a callback twice (#995). A replica now stores a replicated durable's deadline the same way, and a repo or durable ttl sent over the replication stream is capped at what a nanosecond count can hold instead of overflowing. A time far in the past (a year-0000 `at time`, which now parses to the clock's minimum, or any year before about 1733) is refused as older than the retention window; subtracting it from now used to overflow and read the newest version.

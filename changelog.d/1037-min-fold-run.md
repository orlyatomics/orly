- **Changed**: a disk merge folds only if some key in it has a run of more than `--min_fold_run` entries that are not assignments (default 64, the bound the fold cap already allows). The post-merge fold rewrote the whole merged file, every key of it, whenever a single entry in it was not an assignment, and took about three quarters of a merge's CPU, so under sustained ingest merges fell behind and disk layers piled up. A shorter run stays unfolded, still correct, and a later merge folds it once it passes the minimum; `--min_fold_run=0` folds every such merge, as before. On a write-heavy benchmark the merge cost per key fell from 31.8 to 4.3 µs, and disk layers under ingest from up to 20 to at most 5 (#1037).

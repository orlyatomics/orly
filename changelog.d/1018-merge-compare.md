- **Fixed (performance)**: A range read now spends about 40% fewer cycles per row merging its layers. The merge copies each input's tuple key elements once, when the input moves to the key, and then orders keys element by element without pinning either note. It skips equal self-ordered elements with a memcmp. The merge heap keeps its smaller root child, so an input that stays in front costs one comparison instead of a sift. A disk layer's range walk finds the rank where the range ends once, by galloping, instead of placing every row against the range. In a 64-session closed loop of 1,000-row integer ranges, in A B B A order, cycles per read fell from 6.4M to 3.76M quiet and from 9.7M to 5.7M under ingest, and instructions per read fell from 12.9M to 7.25M and from 17.4M to 9.7M. Reads per second rose from 2,770 to 3,020 quiet and from 1,850 to 2,810 under ingest. Page reads rose from 2,700 to 3,990 per second quiet and from 920 to 1,720 under ingest ([#1018](https://github.com/orlyatomics/orly/issues/1018)).

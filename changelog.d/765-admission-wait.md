- **Changed**: memory admission now waits a bounded time for room before it refuses a write. A write that would take the Update or Update Entry pool into the reserve kept for merges used to get `insufficient_memory` at once; it now waits, first come first served, up to `--admission_wait_ms` (default 500, 0 restores the old behaviour) for the merges and Tetris promotions to free room, and is refused only if none comes. A waiting write holds no pool blocks, no admission and no lock (its fiber is parked), and it is woken by the release that makes room, not by polling: the writer at the head of the queue arms a watch on the pool that refused it, and the first block freed, admission released or copy claim released that reaches the level that would admit it wakes the queue. Waiters are admitted by the same check as before, so writers still never use the merges' reserve. A graceful stop refuses every waiting write first. The reporting port's `Memory Admission` line gains `waiting`, `waited`, `wait timeouts`, `refused at stop` and `longest wait`. The batch-backlog and pool-pressure smokes report p50/p99 latency of accepted writes; the batch-backlog smoke prints every failed check, and its CI negative control must now fail on the backlog cap itself, since the control's batches now wait instead of being refused. The memory-full smoke runs `orlyi` with `--admission_wait_ms=5` (`ADMISSION_WAIT_MS`), because at the default the merges always made room in time and nothing was refused, and it now requires refusals that came after the wait ran out. Measured on native arm64 with the CI runner's 4-core topology and #754's faster memory-layer inserts (which let writers outrun the root's memory merge), the batch-backlog smoke went from 11.1% of batches refused to 0, at 564 instead of 476 accepted batches/s, with p99 batch latency 52 ms instead of 32 ms (master: 120 ms); on master alone nothing waits and nothing changes (#765).

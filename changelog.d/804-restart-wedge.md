- **Fixed**: right after a restart on a disk-backed store, `orlyi` could stop acknowledging writes for good, and a SIGTERM then hung before the Tetris wait (about once per 40-kill campaign, always 28 or more restarts in). The durable manager (sessions and POVs) held its `std::mutex` while it looked an id up in its disk files (`New()` checks the id is unused, `Open()` loads it). With nothing cached after a restart, that look is a disk read, which parks the fiber until the read completes, so its runner thread ran other fibers meanwhile. The next of those to create or open a session or POV blocked the thread on the mutex its own thread held, and the parked fiber could never resume. Stacks showed the mutex's owner was the very thread waiting on it, and every client fiber, the Tetris player and connection teardown were queued behind it. The lookups now run without the mutex. A lookup that races a session or POV being saved and dropped from the cache looks again, so it can't bring back an older copy (#804).
- **Added**: the kill campaign fails a kill whose restarted server acknowledged fewer than 100 transactions in a load of at least 1 s (`--wedge-min-txns`, `--wedge-after`), so a SIGKILL run catches a wedge too. Before the kill, and when a SIGTERM stop hangs, it saves every `orlyi` thread's state, wait channel, syscall, the owner of any mutex a thread is blocked on, and `eu-stack` (or `gdb`) stacks into the work directory. It finds `orlyi` by its own PID and checks `/proc/<pid>/comm` (#804).

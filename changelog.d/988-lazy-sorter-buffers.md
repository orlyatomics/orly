- **Changed**: the in-memory sorters behind data-file builds, merges and folds grow their buffers as values arrive (from 1,024 values, doubling) instead of allocating their full size up front, and the fold keeps its output entries in a deque rather than a doubling vector. Most sorters hold far fewer values than their size, and a merge or fold builds one per index and tuple field, so this cut what merges hold while they run. Spills still happen at exactly the same point, and the bytes written are unchanged: a new test pins a fold's output to the bytes master wrote. With a 6 GiB memory budget and a read ladder of 1 to 256 sessions over about 400 disk files, two runs each went from a 7.95 and 8.06 GiB peak RSS to 7.48 and 7.46 GiB, and the heap in use once the ladder stopped from 7.6 and 8.1 GiB to 6.7 and 6.9 GiB. The peak is still over the budget, because merge working memory is still outside the memory plan (#988).

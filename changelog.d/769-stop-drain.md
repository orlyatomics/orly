- **Fixed**: a graceful stop (SIGTERM or SIGINT) no longer loses acknowledged writes, or runs past its deadline, when the POVs' backlogs hold more than Tetris promotes in 30 s (#769). The stop gave Tetris a fixed 30 s to promote every POV's backlog to the global POV, but the backlogs are capped for memory (1/32 of the Update pool each, #721), not for drain time, and on a loaded box Tetris promotes a few hundred updates a second. In an arm64 Docker VM, `tests/graceful_stop_test.sh` on master lost acknowledged writes on every stop (6,414 to 20,150 per stop) and two of three stops hung past 60 s; the flush then also hit its own 30 s bound, chasing promotions that kept arriving. Now a stopping server refuses every write that hasn't committed yet with `insufficient_memory` (retryable; the reason says the server is stopping), and waits for Tetris for as long as it keeps promoting, up to the new `--stop_promote_budget_s` (default 300 s), logging what is left every 5 s; it gives up early only if Tetris promotes nothing for 30 s. If it gives up, it halts promotion first and logs at `LOG_ERR` exactly how many acknowledged updates will be lost (it matched the clients' count to the transaction in testing), and the flush no longer chases Tetris. With the fix, every stop in the same VM finished (72-88 s for about 13,000 unpromoted updates) and lost nothing. The reporting port's `Writer Backlog` line now shows what a stop would have to promote (`unpromoted U updates / E entries; promoted P`), the repo flush's give-up is logged at `LOG_ERR`, and the graceful-stop test's last stop is a long-load stop that writes until the backlogs are 75% full, whatever the clients' speed. Give `docker stop --time` (10 s by default) at least the budget; see `docs/durability.md`.

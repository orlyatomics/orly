- **Fixed**: Tetris promoted only ~100–200 updates a second on a disk-backed server in an arm64 Docker VM, and ~650 on an x86 runner, even with no writers, so a full set of POV backlogs took a minute or more to drain, and a graceful stop under load took as long (#801). 88–93% of each promotion was one durable save: every promotion opened the writer's session to queue its `Accepted` notification and dropped it again, and once the client had gone (as in a graceful stop, which closes every connection first) dropping it saved the session to the durable store and waited for the save, blocking the Tetris thread. Most of the rest was the runner yield between rounds, one round per promotion. Now a Tetris turn plays rounds back to back for up to 5 ms, each still with its own snapshot, assertion replay and committed transaction, so it promotes exactly what as many separate rounds would; and the sessions hear about the turn's promotions once, after the commits, through a pin the player keeps while it promotes (dropped once a turn promotes nothing, on a pause, and at least once a second, so a closed session is still saved). In the VM, 12,000 queued updates in eight POVs now drain in about a second instead of 112 s on disk and 11 s in memory. A new smoke, `clients/smoke/run-tetris-drain.sh`, checks that paused backlogs drain exactly, that a pause mid-drain holds, that guarded writes survive their replay, and that of eight competing claims exactly one is promoted; `import_replication.test` checks that Accepted notifications still reach a connected client and a client that resumes its session after its updates were promoted.

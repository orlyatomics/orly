- **Fixed**: when its update pools fill up, `orlyi` now refuses writes instead of failing them with `bad_alloc` or crashing, and keeps serving reads. Writes wait in the Update and Update Entry pools until merges flush them, and a merge needs room in the same pools to work. `orlyi` now keeps `--memory_reserve_pct` of each pool (default 25%) for the merges and Tetris promotions. It refuses a write that would use that reserve before the write allocates anything, replying with `"status": "insufficient_memory"` over WebSocket, or an error starting `insufficient memory` over the binary protocol. Reads, new sessions and new POVs are never refused, and writes are accepted again on their own once the merges catch up. `--memory_reserve_pct=0` turns this off. The TS, Python and Go clients raise a dedicated error (`InsufficientMemoryError`, `InsufficientMemory`, `ErrInsufficientMemory`), and the reporting port gains a `Memory Admission` line. While admission is on, writes no longer wait up to 5 s when the pools are past half full, because admission now keeps the merges' room. That wait had held each writer to one batch per 5 s and stalled reads for as long. On a fiber, `TPool::Alloc` no longer sleeps its thread for up to 2 s on a miss; it fails at once. The callers that can wait retry where they hold no lock: the memory merge rolls back and is queued again, and the Tetris player replays the round. The memory merge no longer aborts after a failed allocation. A failed mapping update now undoes itself, the disk layer for the file it writes is allocated before the file, and an allocation failure while writing that file is retried like a full disk. A memory merge or a Tetris promotion now claims every block its copy will take before it copies anything, and waits holding nothing if the claim isn't granted. So two copies never each hold part of what they need while waiting for the rest, which could leave the pools full for good. If the global repo can't get a claim for combining its memory layers, it writes its oldest layer on its own, which copies nothing, so it can always flush what Tetris promotes into it. A write with more entries than half the reserve is rejected outright (`write too large`), since one that size might never be promoted. When Tetris promotes an update, the child's memory merge is now queued. Before, a child whose backlog Tetris drained after its last merge kept the released copies in the pools until its next write, and with admission on, that write could be refused for good. The importer now flushes before it reaches the reserve. Also fixed: a copy of an update that ran out of Update Entry pool came back empty instead of failing. A Tetris promotion then moved an empty update into the parent, losing its data, and the global repo's next flush segfaulted (#624). Measured on native arm64 with 8 writers sending 200-write batches to small pools (5,000 updates and 10,000 entries): master segfaulted in both runs, after 64 and 23 batches. With the fix, 2,734 and 4,383 batches went through in 30 s, 2,844 and 4,177 were refused, no write failed any other way, and the slowest read took 132 ms. Single-write throughput shows no consistent change in paired runs: 2,473, 2,872 and 3,200 writes/s against master's 2,032, 2,753 and 2,786 at K=8, and 440, 755 and 819 against 694, 738 and 989 at K=1. A new CI smoke, `clients/smoke/run-memory-full.sh`, requires refusals rather than errors, and its negative control (`ADMISSION=off`) must fail. The #584 pool-pressure smoke now retries refusals as backpressure and reports how many there were (#607).
